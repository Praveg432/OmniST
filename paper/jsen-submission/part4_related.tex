\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Dynamic and inductive forecasting.} Fixed-topology STGCNs~\cite{yu2018stgcn} couple weights to a sensor
count. Inductive kriging~\cite{ignnk2020} reconstructs the signal at unobserved locations from the concurrent
observations of other sensors, a different task from forecasting at a new sensor from its own history. STFO~\cite{stfo2026} and cold-start forecasting at unseen
sensors~\cite{coldstart_unseen2026} target point prediction under growing networks. Our control (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) suggests that what they share, no sensor-specific parameters, is what makes a model applicable at a new sensor.  Kriging protocols can leak spatial information~\cite{yang2026leakagefree}; ours keep held-out sensors out of fitting.

\textbf{Conformal prediction on graphs, space and time.} Split
conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023conformal} has been extended to correlated time
series~\cite{corel2025}, graph time series~\cite{conformalgraphts2026,incontextresidual2026}, dynamic GNNs~\cite{validcpdyngnn2024,nonexchcptgnn2025}, stream networks~\cite{staci2025} and online graph Gaussian processes~\cite{conformalgp_online2025}, mostly with a fixed node set. Zargarbashi and
Bojchevski~\cite{conformalinductivegnn2024} analyze the inductive case in which new nodes shift the scores of
message-passing GNNs. Locality (Lemma~\ref{lem:locality}) removes that interference by construction, which is not
the same as establishing exchangeability.

The closest statistics treat grouped data. Hierarchical conformal prediction
(HCP)~\cite{lee2023hcp} gives distribution-free sets for a new observation from a previously unseen group. With the
same number of points per sensor, HCP's weighted quantile coincides exactly with the sensor-level rule of
Corollary~\ref{cor:pooled} (checked numerically in the released tests), so the rule itself is not new. What differs is the statement: HCP covers one observation of the new group and uses exchangeability within it, whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage} bounds the average over a sensor's points and needs only exchangeability of sensors. Generalized HCP~\cite{mallick2026ghcp} also uses the first observations of the new
group; we have not evaluated it. Sequential
whitening~\cite{sen2026whitening} gives exact coverage under a correct working covariance, and model-free valid
spatial prediction~\cite{validspatial2020} uses the random-location design that our (A1) borrows. Non-exchangeable and
covariate-shift extensions~\cite{barber2023nexcp,tibshirani2019covshift} are the tools for new sensors that differ
systematically from the calibration sensors. What this paper adds is not a conformal method: it is the
sensor-as-group view applied to forecasting at new sensors, a locality property that makes sensor-level scores well
defined, and measurements of the price of each guarantee and of what is lost at forecast time.
