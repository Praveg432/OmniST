Deployed sensor networks change: stations are added, fail, or go offline, and forecasters built on a fixed adjacency matrix cannot forecast at a sensor added after training. We present OmniST, a forecaster with no dependence on the number of sensors, and ask when conformal intervals at a new sensor can be trusted. With neighbors drawn from the training network, a new sensor's error does not depend on other new sensors. Two guarantees then apply. A correction counted in calibration sensors, as in hierarchical conformal prediction, gives expected coverage of at least $1-\alpha$ at a randomly drawn new sensor, but calibrates on the window it evaluates. Adaptive conformal inference run per sensor gives coverage at forecast time for every sensor in the long run, and over any block of windows, with slack of order $1/T$. Exact single-window or location-conditional coverage is impossible without assumptions. On five real networks (12 to 325 sensors) the first held in every finite case at 1.1 to 1.6 times the pooled width. At forecast time the usual pooled recipe left the worst sensor covered only 0.53 to 0.81 of the time; per-sensor adaptive calibration raised it to 0.89 on both traffic networks, certified at 0.81 to 0.85, at no larger median width. It is weak where histories are short: Delhi reaches 0.855. On 70 weather stations installed after training, every station was covered at least 0.88 (certified 0.66), although that forecaster is worse than persistence. A coordinate-free GRU matched OmniST's accuracy within about 2\%.
