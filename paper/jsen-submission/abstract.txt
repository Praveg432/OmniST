Deployed sensor networks change: stations are added, fail, or go offline, and forecasters built on a fixed adjacency matrix cannot forecast at a sensor added after training. We present OmniST, a forecaster with no dependence on the number of sensors, and ask when conformal intervals at a new sensor can be trusted. With neighbors drawn from the training network, a new sensor's error does not depend on other new sensors. Two guarantees then apply. A correction counted in calibration sensors, as in hierarchical conformal prediction, gives expected coverage of at least $1-\alpha$ at a randomly drawn new sensor, but calibrates on the window it evaluates. Adaptive conformal inference run per sensor gives coverage at forecast time for every individual sensor in the long run, with slack of order $1/T$ over $T$ windows. On five real networks (12 to 325 sensors) over repeated random sensor splits, the first held in every finite case at 1.1 to 1.6 times the pooled width with 11 to 89 calibration sensors. At forecast time the usual pooled recipe left the worst sensor covered only 0.53 to 0.81 of the time; per-sensor adaptive calibration raised it to 0.89 on both traffic networks, certified at 0.81 to 0.85 per sensor, at no larger median width, with 3\% of windows infinite. It is weak where histories are short: Delhi reaches 0.855. Sensors farthest from the network fared like the rest. A coordinate-free shared GRU matched OmniST's accuracy within about 2\%, and a coordinate-normalization defect in our earlier code is reported and fixed.
